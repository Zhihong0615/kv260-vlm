# RM11 Worker C — Narrow prior-art audit

**Scope.** Only mechanisms named in the RM11 brief: FPGA interleaved FP accumulation, split-K, dual-dataflow GEMM, orientation-adaptive systolic/dataflow, ViT FPGA FFN, VLM FPGA vision compute, and shared FFN hardware. This is a bounded mechanism audit, not a proof of absence.

## Decision summary

The generic mechanisms are already present in FPGA and VLM work. Do not claim novelty for multi-accumulator recurrence removal, K partition plus reduction, selectable GEMM dataflow, shape-based dataflow choice, or a shared linear/FFN unit alone. The plausible remaining difference is an **empirical system result**: a single RM10-class mixed-precision engine (`F16 weights × F32 activations → F32 accumulation`) covering MiniCPM-V vision FFN-up and FFN-down on KV260, with measured per-call boundaries and full-request effect. That is a workload/platform/arithmetic boundary until a multi-mode datapath beats strong tuned controls.

RM05 gives the exact N=1120 shapes: up `K/M/N=1152/4304/1120`, down `4304/1152/1120`; each is 5.553 GMAC/call, but the CPU's five-call times were 7.156 s and 20.628 s. RM10 has board evidence for down; up's available result in the RM10 record is an out-of-context projection, not a board measurement. Therefore the up/down orientation hypothesis remains open, not demonstrated.

## Mechanism-by-mechanism comparison

| RM11 mechanism | What the primary work solves and why it works | Coverage of RM11 candidate; substantive gap |
|---|---|---|
| **Interleaved FP accumulation** | Marongiu & Palazzari use `L` independent partial scalar products, with `L` matching the pipelined MADD latency, and sum the partials at the end. Each lane is revisited only after its recurrence latency, hiding the feedback dependency. [ISC 2020 paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7295354/) | Directly covers RM10's recurrence-decoupling principle. Accumulator count/order and final reduction are implementation choices, not novelty. The paper does not certify RM11's floating-point error gate or current K26 mapping; those remain required measurements. |
| **Split-K / partial reduction** | FPGA reduction work treats pipelined FP dot products as streams and develops striding/tree schedules to avoid stalls, trading adders, buffering, and latency. [Zhuo et al., IEEE TPDS 2007](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPDS.2007.1068), [Sun & Zambreno, FPT 2009](https://doi.org/10.1109/FPT.2009.5377624). Marongiu's independent partial products are the same K-reduction decomposition at dot-product granularity. | A split-K implementation may expose parallelism when ordinary output tiles underfill the fabric, but partial-state storage and a final reduction add cost; a design that spills partials may also add memory traffic. FP summation order changes. The RM11 gap is whether that trade pays for this exact shape, arithmetic, resource ceiling, and request boundary—not the partition/reduce operation itself. |
| **Dual-dataflow GEMM** | FlexViT maps FC and convolution to one INT8 GEMM engine and switches between input-broadcast and weight-broadcast, choosing the mode from each layer's dimensions; depth-first tiling finishes accumulation in one pass. It reports PYNQ-Z2 board and end-to-end ViT measurements. [FlexViT, FPL 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31938) | Direct precedent for unified GEMM plus runtime-selectable dataflow on a resource-limited edge FPGA. It does not test MiniCPM-V's up/down pair or mixed F16×F32→F32 arithmetic. Those are a concrete measurement boundary, not a new dual-mode principle. |
| **Orientation-adaptive systolic/dataflow** | ReSA supports input-, weight-, and output-stationary modes, spatially splits an array to reduce PE fragmentation, and searches a preferred mode for each tensor before encoding the mode in an instruction. [Lee & Yeh, ACM TACO 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3653363) | A shape lookup that routes tall/wide matrices to known kernels is already an established control. The RM11 candidate is uncovered only if a genuinely shared/multi-mode implementation has a measured resource/utilization/latency advantage over the best fixed map and separately optimized static up/down maps. ReSA's tiny-tensor/DNN results do not establish that advantage for the RM11 pair. |
| **ViT FPGA FFN** | Edge-MoE runs an end-to-end HLS ViT-family accelerator on ZCU102. It uses a unified linear module across MLP and other linear layers, and identifies GELU/softmax resource cost. [Edge-MoE, ICCAD 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18691) FlexViT covers FC layers in an edge ViT with a unified GEMM engine. | FPGA acceleration and unification of ViT FFN/linear computation are not new. Their quantized/fixed-point formats, boards, and model workloads differ from RM11; the remaining result is exact mixed-precision MiniCPM-V integration and its measured end-to-end payoff. |
| **VLM FPGA vision compute** | UniVLM is an end-to-end SmolVLM2 system on KV260: it shares a static main GEMM between ViT and LLM, and streams/interleaves MLP up/gate work into SiLU/down. Its W5A8 integer path and shared-resource ablation show cross-stage sharing on the same board/model family. [AICAS 2026 proceedings portal](https://2026.ieee-aicas.org/publication/), paper pp. 451–455. HI-APP targets quantized CLIP-ViTs with hardware-friendly GELU/LayerNorm approximations, addressing nonlinear cost. [HI-APP, DATE 2026](https://doi.org/10.23919/DATE69613.2026.11539556) | FPGA vision compute, VLM vision+language integration, and shared VLM matrix hardware are already demonstrated. UniVLM does not establish RM11's MiniCPM-V mixed-precision path. HI-APP focuses on quantized nonlinear operators, not RM10's FP32-accumulating GEMM. |
| **Shared FFN accelerator** | Edge-MoE consolidates linear layers with variable dimensions/input sparsity and optional GELU into one run-time-configurable unit. Lu et al. partition Transformer matrices so MHA and FFN share most hardware. [Edge-MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18691), [Lu et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08605). UniVLM further shares GEMM resources across ViT and LLM and streams MLP projections. | Reusing one compute block for up/down is anticipated at the mechanism level. A real gap can be a measured area/performance trade specific to the opposed up/down matrix orientations and F16×F32→F32; it cannot be the fact of sharing alone. |

## VLM and arithmetic boundaries

The closest system papers establish why claims need tight boundaries. VersaVLM reports a KV260/SmolVLM2 system with separate prefill/decode overlays and charges the overlay change; it is a strong static system control, not a shared up/down engine. [AICAS 2026 proceedings portal](https://2026.ieee-aicas.org/publication/), paper pp. 436–440. ReCoVLM maps visual encoding and cross-modal prefill to GPU and FPGA decode, so it establishes VLM stage assignment and FPGA VLM execution, but does not claim FPGA vision encoding. [FCCM 2026 paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/FCCM68464.2026.00026). UniVLM is the closer FPGA-vision/system precedent, as above.

The numeric contract remains a valid *measurement distinction*: the RM11 engine uses F16 weights, F32 activations, and F32 reduction, while FlexViT and UniVLM report INT8 and W5A8 paths, respectively, and HI-APP addresses quantized CLIP. A different precision or VLM does not create an architecture novelty by itself. The contribution boundary is the verified combination of exact arithmetic and numerical tolerance, request-real tensor orientations, board-side PL-call cost, resource fit, and full MiniCPM-V effect.

## Controls required before an architecture claim

If measurements show up/down prefer different mappings, compare on the same inputs and board:

1. One best fixed dataflow for both operators.
2. Independently tuned static up and down dataflows, with both same-bitstream mode selection and a two-overlay variant where relevant; charge mode/overlay change and every host/PL boundary.
3. The proposed shared/multi-mode hardware, reporting resource use, PE utilization, kernel time, total PL-call time, combined FFN time, and full-request wall time.

**Conclusion:** no candidate mechanism in this audit is novel by itself. `UNIFIED_FFN_SYSTEM_PAPER` is supported if one engine simply covers both operators and the main result is measured end-to-end co-design. `GO_ORIENTATION_ADAPTIVE_ARCHITECTURE` needs new evidence that the opposed orientations have materially different optimal dataflows *and* that shared multi-mode hardware beats both the best fixed and two-static-engine/overlay controls at similar resources and request boundary cost. A shape-keyed dispatcher alone does not clear that bar.
